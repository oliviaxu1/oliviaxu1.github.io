## Research Overview

This study investigates how household emergency-supplies storage intentions are formed and how those intentions translate into actual preparedness behavior during public health emergencies. Drawing on the Theory of Planned Behavior and the intention–behavior gap, the research examines the effects of attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and community-level contextual factors on household preparedness.

The study develops a two-stage framework covering both intention formation and behavior transformation. It considers community institutional trust and community networks as contextual factors that may influence whether residents convert their preparedness intentions into actual storage behavior.

## Research Motivation

Households are an important part of the wider public-health emergency-response system, yet many residents do not maintain adequate emergency supplies. Although individuals may express a strong intention to prepare for emergencies, this intention does not always translate into actual behavior.

Existing research had largely focused on panic buying and preparedness for natural disasters, while relatively limited attention had been given to routine household emergency-supplies storage. This study therefore examines both the formation of preparedness intentions and the contextual factors influencing the intention–behavior gap.

## Key Findings

- Subjective norms and perceived behavioral control had significant positive effects on residents’ intentions to store household emergency supplies.
- Perceived behavioral control also positively influenced actual storage behavior, whereas attitude did not have a statistically significant effect on storage intention.
- Storage intention positively affected behavior, but the relatively modest relationship indicated the existence of a meaningful intention–behavior gap.
- Community institutional trust negatively moderated the transformation of storage intention into behavior, potentially because stronger institutional trust increased residents’ reliance on community support.
- Community networks positively moderated the intention–behavior relationship by strengthening communication, information exchange, and social influence among residents.
